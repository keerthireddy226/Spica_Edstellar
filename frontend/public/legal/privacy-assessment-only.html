This notice is for people who take only an English assessment on Spica, without using its practice or lessons. That happens in two ways, and who is responsible differs:

  • Part A: you received a test link from an employer, recruiter or admissions office (a 'candidate').
  • Part B: you bought an assessment yourself and took it without preparing on Spica.

Part A: tests from an employer, recruiter or admissions office

The organization that sent you the link is the controller of your test data (in India, the "data fiduciary"). Edstellar, which runs Spica, is their processor and uses your data only to run the test and give them the result, under a Data Processing Agreement. That organization should tell you how it uses results in its hiring or admissions decision.

  • Who receives your results: the organization that invited you: your level, skill scores, answers, the integrity review, and a report it may share with its hiring or admissions team.
  • How long: the organization sets how long candidate data is kept (by default 180 days; between 30 days and 3 years). Proctoring media follows the rule in the 'How long' section below.
  • Training: your data is never used to train or test Spica's marking.
  • Requests about your data: contact the organization that invited you; Spica will help them. You can also contact us.

Part B: an assessment you bought yourself

Edstellar is the controller, exactly as in the Privacy Notice for individual users (paid plan), which applies in full. Your certificate shows your name, level, date and number, and anyone with its link or number can check it is genuine. Using your answers to improve Spica's marking is optional and off unless you turn it on.

What is collected during the assessment

  • Your name and email, from the invitation or your account.
  • Your answers: spoken (recordings), written, and the scores.
  • Proctoring data, only for proctored tests: camera photos during the test (kept only when something looks wrong, plus an occasional sample), screenshots of your shared screen (when you leave the test, and every few minutes), a short room video, a photo of your ID and of your face, a short voice sample, and events such as leaving the test tab. Before every test you are shown exactly what that test collects, and you agree on that screen.
  • Face and voice templates (number patterns made from your face and voice) to check that the same person takes the whole test. They are made only if you choose face and voice matching; you can choose a manual identity check instead, and withdraw at any time.
  • A device check: internet speed, microphone and speakers.
  • Technical data: IP address, browser and times, to keep the test secure.

How you are scored

Your spoken and written answers are sent to AI platforms to be marked, under paid business terms that do not allow those platforms to use your answers to train their own models. Multiple-choice and fill-in answers are marked by fixed rules. Face and voice matching run on our own servers. Nothing fails you automatically: anything unusual is checked by a person, and you can ask for a person to review your score. During a test your browser's built-in speech service may also turn your spoken answer into text, as a back-up; that service is run by your browser's maker.

Your result is a recommendation to a person, not an automatic decision. Where a hiring or admissions decision is made using your result, you can ask the organization for a person to review it and to explain the main reasons. In some places (for example the EU AI Act, and New York City's rules on automated hiring tools) employers must tell you that an automated tool is used and give you these rights.

Who receives your data

  • The organization that invited you (Part A), or only you (Part B).
  • Reviewers who check flagged tests.
  • Service providers that work for us under contract and only on our instructions: website delivery, cloud hosting and storage, AI marking, email delivery and payment processing. We do not sell your data and do not share it for advertising.
  • Authorities, only when the law requires it.

Where your data is stored

Your data is stored on secure cloud servers, which may be outside your country. When data leaves your country, we use the safeguards the law requires, such as standard contractual clauses with our providers, and we only transfer it to countries not restricted by your country's law.

How long it is kept

  • Proctoring photos, screenshots, recordings and ID images: 30 days after the test if nothing was flagged. A flagged test keeps them for 90 days, or the period your organization sets, and up to 1 year while it is still under review. Proctoring event logs: 1 year.
  • Face and voice templates: until you withdraw your consent or delete your account.
  • Part A: your result and account as the inviting organization sets (default 180 days). Part B: while your account is open.
  • Data download files you request: 7 days.

Your rights

You have the rights below. For Part A, send requests to the organization that invited you first; Spica helps them. For Part B, contact us directly.

  • See and download your data (Profile → Download my data).
  • Correct your details.
  • Delete your account and data (Profile → Delete my account).
  • Withdraw any consent you gave, such as face and voice matching, at any time. Withdrawing does not affect what was done before.
  • Ask for a person to review an AI score.
  • Ask questions or complain to the Spica Super Admin. We reply within 30 days.

Extra rights in some countries

  • India (Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules): you can ask for a summary of your data and who received it, correct or erase it, and withdraw consent as easily as you gave it. You can contact our Grievance Officer, the Spica Super Admin. If you are not satisfied with our reply, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India. You can name another person to use your rights if you die or cannot act yourself.
  • European Union, EEA and United Kingdom (GDPR, UK GDPR): you can also restrict or object to our use of your data, receive it in a portable format, and not be subject to a decision based only on automated processing that significantly affects you. You can complain to your local data protection authority.
  • United States: we do not sell or share personal information for advertising. Where state law gives you the right (for example California), you can ask what we hold, correct it, delete it and limit the use of sensitive information, and we will not treat you differently for asking. Face and voice data are collected only with your written (click-through) consent, as laws such as Illinois' Biometric Information Privacy Act require.
  • Other countries (for example Singapore, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Brazil, Australia, Canada, South Africa): we honour the rights your local law gives you. Contact us and we will help.

Children

Spica is for people aged 18 and over. When you agree to this notice, you confirm that you are 18 or older. If you believe someone younger has given us data, contact us and we will delete it.

Security

We use encrypted connections, hashed passwords, optional two-step login, access limited by role, and an audit log of who does what. If a data breach affects you, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

Cookies

We use only the cookies and browser storage needed to keep you logged in and remember your settings. We do not use advertising or tracking cookies. Spica's pages load their fonts from an external font service, which receives your device's IP address.

Changes

If we change this notice, we will ask you to read and agree to the new version the next time you use Spica.
