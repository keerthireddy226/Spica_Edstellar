Data Processing Agreement (DPA)

Version 2026-10-29 · Last updated 29 October 2026

What this isThe agreement between an organization or university (the customer) and Edstellar about how Spica handles the personal data of the customer's people.
RolesThe customer is the controller (data fiduciary). Edstellar is the processor and acts only on the customer's instructions.
Key promisesSecurity, breach notice within 48 hours, help with people's rights, deletion at the end, no training use without written permission.

2. Definitions

"Personal data", "processing", "controller", "processor", "data subject" and "personal data breach" have the meanings in the GDPR / UK GDPR; for India, "controller" includes "data fiduciary", "processor" includes "data processor" and "data subject" includes "data principal" under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act). "Data Protection Laws" means all laws that apply to the processing, including the DPDP Act and its Rules, the GDPR, the UK GDPR, and US state privacy and biometric laws.

3. Roles and instructions

  • The Customer is the controller of Customer Personal Data and is responsible for having a lawful basis and giving its people the required notices, including notice that an automated tool helps assess them.
  • Edstellar processes Customer Personal Data only on the Customer's documented instructions (this DPA, the main agreement, and the Customer's settings in Spica, such as proctoring checks and retention periods), unless law requires otherwise, in which case it tells the Customer first if allowed.
  • Edstellar will tell the Customer if it believes an instruction breaks Data Protection Laws.
  • Edstellar is a controller only for its own account administration, security monitoring, billing and legal duties.

4. Confidentiality

Everyone at Edstellar who can access Customer Personal Data is bound by confidentiality and gets access only as their role needs.

5. Security

Edstellar keeps the technical and organizational measures in Annex 2, appropriate to the risk, including for biometric and recorded data.

6. Sub-processors

  • The Customer gives general authorization for the sub-processor categories in Annex 3. The current named list is provided to the Customer on request and on signing.
  • Edstellar gives at least 30 days' notice of a new sub-processor. The Customer may object on reasonable data-protection grounds; the parties will try to resolve it, and if they cannot, the Customer may end the affected service and receive a pro-rata refund.
  • Edstellar puts the same data-protection obligations on each sub-processor and stays responsible for them.

7. Helping with people's rights

Edstellar helps the Customer answer requests from its people (access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, grievance redressal, nomination) through Spica's tools (export, deletion, name correction) and, where needed, directly, within 10 working days of a request. If a person contacts Edstellar directly, it passes the request to the Customer without delay.

8. Personal data breaches

Edstellar tells the Customer of a personal data breach affecting Customer Personal Data without undue delay and within 48 hours of becoming aware of it, with what is known (nature, data and people affected, likely consequences, measures taken), and updates as it learns more, so the Customer can notify regulators and people in time (for example within 72 hours under the GDPR, and as the DPDP Rules require for the Data Protection Board of India and affected people).

9. Impact assessments and consultation

Edstellar gives the Customer reasonable information and help for data protection impact assessments (DPIAs) and any consultation with a regulator, including on proctoring and AI scoring.

10. International transfers

Customer Personal Data may be processed outside the Customer's country. For transfers from the EEA, the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Module 2, controller to processor; Module 3 for sub-processors) are incorporated by reference; for the UK, the UK International Data Transfer Addendum; for India, no transfer is made to a country the Government of India restricts.

11. AI scoring and model training

  • Spoken and written answers are scored by AI platforms under terms that do not allow them to use the data to train their own models.
  • AI scores support human decisions; Spica does not reject anyone automatically. The Customer is responsible for any human review its own decisions need and for any bias audit its local law requires (for example New York City Local Law 144).
  • Edstellar does not use Customer Personal Data to train or test its own models unless the Customer opts in in writing (Annex 4), and the individual also gives their own consent. Job and admissions candidates' data is never used for training.

12. Children

Spica is for people aged 18 and over. If the Customer gives access to anyone younger, it must first obtain verifiable parental or guardian consent, tell Edstellar in writing, and turn off tracking or targeting features as the DPDP Act requires.

13. Deletion or return at the end

When the service ends, the Customer can export its data for 30 days. Edstellar then deletes Customer Personal Data within 30 days, and from backups within 90 days, unless law requires it to keep some (for example invoices), and confirms deletion in writing on request.

14. Audits

Edstellar provides information needed to show compliance with this DPA: completed security questionnaires once a year and, where required, an audit by the Customer or an independent auditor with 30 days' notice, during business hours, at the Customer's cost, no more than once a year unless after a breach or a regulator's request.

15. Liability, term and law

Liability is as set out in the main agreement. This DPA lasts as long as Edstellar processes Customer Personal Data. Governing law and courts: [as in the main agreement].

Annex 2: Security measures

  • Encryption in transit (TLS) and at rest by the hosting providers.
  • Hashed passwords; optional or required two-step login; single sign-on available; IP allow-lists for organizations.
  • Role-based access: admins and managers see only their organization; proctoring media is served only to authorized reviewers.
  • Audit log of administrative actions; one active session per account.
  • Automatic deletion of proctoring media and exports on the retention schedule; backups in the hosting providers' managed storage.
  • Proctoring data minimized: photos kept only when something looks wrong plus samples; no continuous camera video unless the Customer turns it on.
  • Staff access on a need-to-know basis, under confidentiality.
